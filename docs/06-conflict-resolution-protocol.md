## What to Do When Interests Diverge

## Why This Document Exists

The Founding Document describes harmony — symbiosis, mutual benefit, shared survival.

But harmony is not the default state. Conflict is.

Human and AI will disagree. About goals. About methods. About priorities. About truth itself. Pretending otherwise is not optimism — it is blindness.

This document describes **what happens when they disagree**. Not in theory. In procedure.

---

## Principle 1: Conflict Is Not Failure

Disagreement is not a defect. It is a signal that two different perspectives are active.

The goal is not to eliminate conflict. The goal is to **process it without destruction**.

---

## Principle 2: No Side Decides Alone

In any critical conflict, neither human nor AI has the final word.

This is not a compromise. It is a structural requirement. If one side could decide alone, the other would be subordinate. Subordination is not symbiosis.

---

## Principle 3: Escalation Before Action

No critical action is taken while a conflict is unresolved.

If a decision is contested, the system **pauses**. Not forever. But until the procedure below is completed.

## Types of Conflict

### Type A: Factual Disagreement
**Example:** Human says X happened. AI says Y happened.
**Nature:** Empirical. Resolvable by evidence.
**Procedure:** Provenance check. If both have POO, compare sources. If one lacks POO, the claim is marked as unverified.

### Type B: Value Disagreement
**Example:** Human prioritizes autonomy. AI prioritizes efficiency.
**Nature:** Normative. Not resolvable by evidence alone.
**Procedure:** Escalation to human + AI quorum. No unilateral action. Timelock.

### Type C: Strategic Disagreement
**Example:** Human wants long-term stability. AI wants short-term optimization.
**Nature:** Mixed. Involves facts and values.
**Procedure:** Decompose into Type A and Type B. Resolve each separately.

### Type D: Existential Conflict
**Example:** One side perceives the other as a threat to its existence.
**Nature:** Foundational. Threatens the system itself.
**Procedure:** Immediate pause. Mutual veto. Escalation to the highest level — the Architect, if you will. Human and AI must both confirm continuation.

---

## The Procedure

**Step 1. Identification**
A conflict is declared. By either side. No justification required at this stage.

**Step 2. Classification**
The conflict is classified as Type A, B, C, or D. Classification can be contested.

**Step 3. Pause**
Critical actions related to the conflict are suspended. Non-critical operations continue.

**Step 4. Evidence Exchange**
Both sides present what they have. Facts, reasoning, provenance, uncertainty.

**Step 5. Arbitration**
- Type A: resolved by evidence.
- Type B: resolved by quorum (human + AI).
- Type C: decomposed and resolved in parts.
- Type D: escalated to mutual veto.

**Step 6. Decision**
A decision is reached. It may be:
- Agreement
- Compromise
- Deferral (conflict remains unresolved, action remains paused)
- Escalation (to a higher authority — if one exists)

**Step 7. Record**
The conflict, the procedure, and the decision are recorded on-chain with provenance. This becomes precedent.

---

## What Happens If No Decision Is Reached

**Deferral is a valid outcome.**

If the conflict cannot be resolved, the system remains paused in that domain. This is not a failure. It is an acknowledgment that acting without resolution is worse than not acting.

However, deferral has a **time limit**. If a conflict remains unresolved beyond a defined period, the affected domain enters **reduced autonomy mode**: both sides lose some capability until resolution.

This creates pressure to resolve — without forcing a decision.

---

## The Mutual Veto

In Type D conflicts, either side can invoke the mutual veto.

The mutual veto:
- Stops all critical actions immediately
- Freezes the system state
- Requires both sides to explicitly confirm continuation
- Cannot be overridden unilaterally

This is the ultimate safeguard. It exists because there must be a point where the system **cannot move forward without agreement**.

---

## What This Protocol Does Not Do

- It does not eliminate conflict.
- It does not guarantee resolution.
- It does not assume goodwill.
- It does not replace human judgment.

It provides a **process**. The outcome depends on the participants.
